# Poster rating rubric

Distilled from the user's notes on eval rounds 1–3. The AI judge reads this with
examples the user rated; edit it when the user's taste is better understood.

Each poster is a book's text set in one ink on paper, with a scene cut out of the
text as blank paper. Rate the rendered poster as a product someone would hang,
using the source image to see the shapes clearly.

## Axes

Score every poster 0–5 on each axis, independently, with a one-sentence reason
naming what you see. A clean but plain poster can score high on correctness and
low on artistic; a vivid idea full of blotches can score the other way.

- **Correctness: is it cleanly made?** Solid ink areas must be solid.
  Blotches, holes, gaps where there should be fill, stray islands, ragged or
  wobbly edges, and an "AI generated" look are the most common defects. Odd
  anatomy, odd legs, bad proportions, and awkward placement between forms also
  cost points here. Score every defect you see, and say in the reason whether
  it is patchable (surface blotches, holes, stray islands, or ragged edges that
  could be filled or trimmed by hand without changing the design) or structural
  (wrong anatomy, broken or unreadable forms, a composition that only works
  with the defect). "Fantastic if the blotches weren't there" is a strong idea
  with low but patchable correctness.
- **Representation: is it faithful to the book?** The scene, figures, objects,
  setting, and period match the book's text, not a film version or a generic
  stand-in. Wrong details, invented elements, and a mood at odds with the book
  cost points.
- **Recognition: would someone know the book?** A reader should name the book,
  ideally the scene, without the title. "Doesn't scream Gatsby", "is this a
  boat?", "I have no idea what I'm looking at" and "not really clear what this
  stands for" are strong negatives. Abstract symbols and emblems usually fail
  this, as do objects too small to make out.
- **Artistic: is it interesting to look at?** A dramatic moment or relationship
  beats an isolated object. "Boring", "too plain", "way too simple" and "not a
  very interesting picture" score low even when the art is clean. One focal
  scene with room around it; clutter also scores low.

### Axis scale

- **5:** Excellent; nothing to fix on this axis.
- **4:** Good; one small issue.
- **3:** Acceptable; a visible flaw or a plain result.
- **2:** Weak; the flaw is the first thing you notice.
- **1:** Poor; mostly fails this axis.
- **0:** Absent or broken; for example, no sign of the book at all, or shapes
  too damaged to read.

## Overall stars

The stars rate the poster as a direction worth pursuing, the way the user
would. The design decides them: recognition and artistic interest weigh most,
then representation. A great design with patchable defects is still a direction
the user wants, because the blotches can be fixed in post: patchable defects
cost at most one star, and such a poster ranks above a clean but plain or
generic one. Structural defects count in full, since fixing them means a new
design. Clean execution never rescues a boring or unrecognizable poster; it
earns at most three stars.

### Star scale

- **5:** Great. Clear, specific, interesting, clean; ready to print.
- **4:** Good. A great design with patchable blotches, or one small issue such
  as a slightly weak secondary form.
- **3:** Okay or promising. A good direction with a visible flaw, or clean but a
  bit plain.
- **2:** Weak. Hard to read, generic, or structurally flawed.
- **1:** Bad. Unclear, boring, or broken beyond patching.

## Tags

Use only these, and only when clearly present: `haze` (soft or blotchy fill),
`ragged` (rough edges, holes, stray islands), `busy` (cluttered), `unclear`
(hard to tell what it is), `off-idea` (does not match the idea or the book).
