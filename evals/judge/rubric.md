# Poster rating rubric

Distilled from the user's notes on eval rounds 1–3. The AI judge reads this with
examples the user rated; edit it when the user's taste is better understood.

Each poster is a book's text set in one ink on paper, with a scene cut out of the
text as blank paper. Rate the rendered poster as a product someone would hang,
using the source image to see the shapes clearly.

## What earns stars

- **Recognizable and specific to this book.** A reader of the book should know
  the scene. "Doesn't scream Gatsby", "is this a boat?", "I have no idea what I'm
  looking at" and "not really clear what this stands for" are strong negatives.
  Abstract symbols and emblems usually fail this.
- **Interesting.** A dramatic moment or relationship beats an isolated object.
  "Boring", "too plain", "way too simple" and "not a very interesting picture"
  cost stars even when the art is clean.
- **Clean solid shapes.** Solid ink areas must be solid. Blotches, holes, gaps
  where there should be fill, stray islands, ragged or wobbly edges, and an
  "AI generated" look are the most common defects. A strong idea with blotches
  still loses at least one star ("fantastic if the blotches weren't there").
- **Sound drawing.** Good proportions and believable forms. Odd anatomy, odd
  legs, objects too small to recognize, and awkward placement between forms
  cost stars.
- **Not busy.** One focal scene with room around it. Clutter costs stars.

## Scale

- **5:** Great. Clear, specific, interesting, clean; ready to print.
- **4:** Good. One small issue, such as a slightly weak secondary form.
- **3:** Okay or promising. A good direction with a visible flaw, or clean but a
  bit plain.
- **2:** Weak. Hard to read, generic, or noticeably blotchy.
- **1:** Bad. Unclear, boring, or broken.

## Tags

Use only these, and only when clearly present: `haze` (soft or blotchy fill),
`ragged` (rough edges, holes, stray islands), `busy` (cluttered), `unclear`
(hard to tell what it is), `off-idea` (does not match the idea or the book).
